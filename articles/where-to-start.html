Getting started

Learning Arabic as an adult: where to start

By Dr. Ahmed Bekhet · 5 minute read

Many adults put off learning Arabic for years because it looks impossible: a new alphabet, a new direction, sounds that don't exist in English. The good news is that the first step is much smaller than it looks. Here is the path that works for most beginners.

1. Start with the letters, not the grammar

Arabic has 28 letters. Most of them will feel familiar within two or three weeks of short, daily practice. Once you can sound out words, everything else (Quranic Arabic, conversation, vocabulary) gets easier, because you can read what you are learning.

Tip: Learn letters in families that share a shape. ب, ت and ث look the same except for their dots, so you learn three letters for the price of one.

2. Learn the three short vowels

Arabic writes short vowels as small marks above or below the letter: fatha (a), kasra (i) and damma (u). With just these three marks you can read كَتَبَ (kataba, “he wrote”) on your own.

3. Practise a little, every day

Fifteen minutes a day beats two hours once a week. Read a line, listen to it, and say it out loud. Your mouth needs practice as much as your eyes do.

4. Connect it to something you love

Choose a goal that matters to you: understanding your prayers, reading the Quran yourself, or talking with family. A clear reason keeps you going on busy weeks.
